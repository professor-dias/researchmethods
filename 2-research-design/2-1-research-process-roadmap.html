The Nine-Step Journey Down the Research Highway

So how do you actually produce one of those pixels? Researchers Crosby, Salazar, and DiClemente describe it as a road trip — a straightforward-looking path that can still take turns, hit detours, and occasionally dead-end. Nine steps, in order, get you there.

1
Choosing Your Destination
Define exactly who your research population is — not "teenagers," but "adolescents aged 13–19 in areas with dense tobacco sales."
2
Mapping Your Route
Turn a broad goal into a precise, narrow research question. Narrow questions are what make rigorous designs possible.
3
Choosing Your Ride
Decide: will you observe people as they naturally are, or intervene and measure what changes? (More in 2.2.)
4
Which Lane Are You In?
Pick the specific design that answers your question rigorously without wasting time or resources you don't need.
5
Stopping for Snacks
Decide exactly which variables you need to measure to answer the question — no more, no fewer.
6
Do You Take the Bypass?
Choose how you'll actually recruit and sample participants from that population.
7
Is Autopilot a Good Idea?
Implement the plan — with fidelity. This is where careful planning either pays off or quietly falls apart.
8
Arriving at Your Destination
Analyze the data you collected — the whole point of the trip.
9
Sharing Your Vacation Photos
Disseminate the findings — publish, present, share back with the community you studied. An unshared study never becomes a pixel anyone else can use.
Two planning frameworks you'll see referenced in the literature 

RE-AIM evaluates a health program across five dimensions: Reach (who actually participates), Effectiveness (does it work in the real world, not just in theory), Adoption (do practitioners and settings take it up), Implementation (is it delivered as designed), and Maintenance (does it stick around). It's often used to plan and evaluate Step 9's real-world impact.

PRECEDE-PROCEED is a broader planning model that works backward from a health outcome, through behavioral and environmental factors, to the specific educational and policy strategies that could change them — useful for framing Step 1 and Step 2 before the nine-step model even begins.

Your Semester Checklist

Here's the same nine steps, translated out of the road-trip metaphor and into exactly what you'll do, in order, every time you plan a study this semester. Come back to this page and check items off as you go.

One nine-step journey produces one pixel: one specific finding, about one specific population, answering one specific question. That can feel small. It isn't — it's exactly how every body of scientific knowledge has ever been built. 2.3 is about the traps that can quietly ruin a well-planned study; 2.4 is how to find and assemble the pixels that already exist before you publish one of your own.
