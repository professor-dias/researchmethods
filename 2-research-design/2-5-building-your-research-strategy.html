Finding the Research Gap
What it is: Something the existing research hasn't answered yet — a population nobody has studied, a relationship nobody has tested, data that's gone out of date, or findings that contradict each other.
How to find one: Read what's already published, then ask: Who wasn't studied? What wasn't measured? What changed since this research was done? Do two studies disagree?

Build your problem statement

Name a specific issue, state who it affects, and point to the gap — that's the "so what" that justifies the whole study.

Hypothesis

The question: What do you expect will happen? (Only for quantitative or mixed methods studies — qualitative studies don't write hypotheses.)
Your study type is currently Qualitative — qualitative studies explore meaning and experience rather than test numeric predictions, so there's no hypothesis to build here.
What a Hypothesis Is
A testable prediction, based on prior research or theory, using numeric data.
Requirements: Clear and concise, testable with data, consistent with the literature, and includes the word "significant."
