Scale Design

Many constructs of interest — anxiety, resilience, job satisfaction — are latent (unobservable directly). Researchers measure them using multi-item scales: multiple related questions whose responses are combined into a composite score.

  • Each item should measure one facet of the construct (unidimensionality)
  • Item responses are typically summed or averaged to create a scale score
  • Some items must be reverse-scored before combining (e.g., "I never feel anxious" in an anxiety scale)
  • Report reliability (e.g., Cronbach's alpha) and validity (e.g., factor structure)

Item 4 is reverse-scored before averaging (a 5 on "I feel calm" counts as a 1 toward anxiety) — otherwise it would cancel out the other three items instead of confirming them.

Likert scales — a common source of confusion: individual Likert items are ordinal. Composite scale scores (the sum or mean of multiple Likert items) are commonly treated as interval-level in practice, particularly in psychology and social science. This is a debated convention — be explicit about your decision.
