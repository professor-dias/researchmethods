Probability vs. non-probability sampling

Click each method to see how it selects from the same population of 100 people.

Probability Methods
Simple random — every unit has an equal chance of being chosen, like drawing names from a hat.
Stratified — the population is split into subgroups (e.g., age groups), then randomly sampled within each so every subgroup is represented.
Cluster — the population is split into natural clusters (e.g., clinics), then whole clusters are randomly selected.
Non-Probability Methods
Convenience — whoever is easiest to reach (e.g., people walking by).
Snowball — existing participants recruit future participants from their own networks.
Purposive — the researcher deliberately selects participants who fit specific criteria.

Show me the math 

n = (Z2 × p × (1−p)) / e2, using the safest assumption that p = 50%. When you enter a known population, that number gets adjusted down with n ÷ (1 + (n−1)/N) — sampling 300 out of 400 people needs a very different answer than sampling 300 out of 400,000.

Rule of thumb: 95% confidence and a ±5% margin of error is the most common combination — it's what most published surveys report.

Older respondents answered far more often than they were represented in the invitation list. Any conclusion drawn from just the respondents will over-represent that group — that's non-response bias, and it can't be fixed by collecting a bigger sample of the same biased respondents.
