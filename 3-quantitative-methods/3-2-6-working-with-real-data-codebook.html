Understanding Your Data Before Analysis

Before running a single line of code, spend time getting to know your dataset. Time invested here prevents errors downstream and forces you to engage with your data as a researcher, not just an operator.

Reading the Codebook
Understand what each variable represents before looking at its values.
Note all missing value codes (e.g., 555 = not applicable, 999 = refused to answer).
Identify pre-existing composite variables and understand how they were scored.
Variable Audit
List all variables and their intended measurement level — cross-reference with Variable Types.
Check actual data types against expected types (e.g., is a numeric-looking variable stored as text?).
Identify variables you will not use — working with a focused subset reduces errors.
Sitting with Your Data
Run simple counts, summaries, and spot-checks before any analysis. Look at raw values. Does the range make sense? Are there impossible values? Are distributions roughly as expected?
Missing Data Patterns
Distinguish between missing completely at random (MCAR), missing at random (MAR), and missing not at random (MNAR) — the distinction has direct implications for how you handle missingness. See Missing Data in Topic 4 for the handling methods themselves.
Quantify missingness per variable.
Assess whether missing patterns are systematic.
Data Quality Checks
Check for duplicates — e.g., duplicate participant IDs.
Check for impossible or out-of-range values.
Check distributions for extreme skew or multimodality that may indicate data collection issues.
Cross-check derived variables against their source variables.
Treat this checklist as your guardrail before every analysis: Codebook → Variable Audit → Sit With It → Missing Data → Quality Checks. Skipping steps here is where most analysis errors actually start.
