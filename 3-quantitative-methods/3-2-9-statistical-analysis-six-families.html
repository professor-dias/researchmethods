Statistical Analysis

Once you know your outcome type and study design (see the Test Selector Tool), you're choosing from one of these six families. Each links to its full deep-dive in Topic 4.

Compare — are groups different?
t-tests (independent or paired) compare means between two groups; ANOVA compares three or more. Mann–Whitney U and Kruskal–Wallis are the non-parametric fallback for ordinal or non-normal data.
→ Full deep-dive: 4.2 Compare
Relate — are variables associated?
Pearson correlation for two continuous variables; Spearman for ordinal or non-normal ones; chi-square for two categorical variables.
→ Full deep-dive: 4.3 Relate
Predict — which variables explain an outcome?
Linear regression for a continuous outcome, logistic regression for a binary one, Poisson or negative binomial for counts.
→ Full deep-dive: 4.4 Predict
Mechanisms & Measurement — why, and are you measuring it right?
Mediation asks if a third variable explains the X→Y relationship; moderation asks if that relationship depends on a third variable. Factor analysis and SEM check the structure behind your measurements.
→ Full deep-dive: 4.5 Explain  ·  Measure
Longitudinal — how does it change over time?
Repeated measures ANOVA for normally distributed outcomes at multiple time points; mixed effects models when you need to account for clustering and individual trajectories.
→ Full deep-dive: 4.8 Longitudinal
Explore — are there hidden patterns?
Clustering (k-means, hierarchical) finds natural groupings; PCA reduces dimensionality; variable importance flags your strongest predictors.
→ Full deep-dive: 4.7 Discover
