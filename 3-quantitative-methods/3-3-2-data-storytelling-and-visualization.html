You want to show…Reach for…
Exact numbers a reader might need to quote (means, coefficients, p-values)A table
How a few categories compareA bar chart
How something changes over timeA line chart
The relationship between two continuous variablesA scatterplot
The spread and outliers in a distributionA boxplot
Table — exact values
Precision
Chart — the pattern
Recognition
Tables and charts aren't competitors — they trade precision for pattern-recognition. A reader spots a trend in a chart in half a second; the same reader needs a table to know the trend was exactly 4.2 percentage points. Good reporting usually uses both: the chart to make the point, the table (often in an appendix) to back it up.

Closing remarks — responsible use of AI. AI tools are powerful accelerants for quantitative research, but they are not substitutes for methodological rigor. Understanding your data, choosing appropriate methods, and interpreting results critically cannot be delegated to a language model. They require domain knowledge, judgment, and accountability that only you, the researcher, can provide.

Use AI to move faster. Use your training to move correctly.
