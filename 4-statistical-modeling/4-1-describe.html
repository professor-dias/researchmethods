The question: What does my data actually look like?

Before you compare anything or run any test, just look at your data plainly. This step alone catches most mistakes early.

Flagship tool: Mean, Median, and a Picture
Why we do this: You need one number that sums up your data, and a picture that shows its shape.
The logic: The mean adds every value up and divides by how many there are. The median lines every value up in order and picks the middle one. A histogram stacks your values into bars so you can see the shape.
What to look for: Do the mean and median sit close together, or far apart? Is the shape roughly symmetric, or does it lean heavily to one side?
What it means: If the mean and median are far apart, a few extreme values are pulling the mean away from where most of your data actually sits. That changes which tests are safe to use later.
