Machine Learning

The question: Can a computer find the pattern for me, without me writing down the formula first?

Traditional stats asks you to pick the shape of the formula in advance. Machine learning lets the computer try many shapes and keep whichever works best.

Flagship idea: Train, then Test
Why we do this: A computer can memorize your exact data perfectly without actually learning anything useful.
The logic: Split your data in two. Let the computer learn patterns from one part (training data). Then check its predictions against the other part it has never seen (test data).
What to look for: How well it does on the training data versus the test data.
What it means: Great on training data but poor on test data means it memorized instead of learning — this is called overfitting. You want good performance on data it has never seen.
Supervised (you have answers to learn from)
Random Forest / Gradient Boosting / XGBoost — many simple decision rules voting together
SVM — finds the clearest dividing line between groups
Neural Networks — layers of simple math units that combine into complex patterns
KNN — predicts based on whichever past cases look most similar
Naive Bayes — predicts using simple, independent probability rules
Unsupervised (no answers given)
K-Means / Hierarchical Clustering — see Discover
PCA — see Discover
Autoencoders — a neural network that compresses data down and rebuilds it, learning the essentials in between
