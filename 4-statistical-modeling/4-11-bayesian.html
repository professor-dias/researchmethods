Bayesian

The question: I already had a belief — how should new evidence update it?

Instead of starting from scratch every time, Bayesian thinking starts with what you already believed and nudges it based on new data.

Flagship idea: Prior → Evidence → Posterior
Why we do this: You rarely start with a truly blank mind — you usually have some reasonable expectation before collecting data.
The logic: Start with your prior belief. Collect data. Blend the two together, giving more weight to whichever one is more certain, to get your updated belief.
What to look for: How far the updated belief moved away from the starting belief, and how much evidence it took to move it.
What it means: Strong new evidence shifts your belief a lot. Weak evidence barely moves it. This is how you naturally update your mind about anything — just made mathematically precise.
