Diagnostics

The question: Can I actually trust the model I just built?

Building a model is easy. Checking whether it's honest is the part people skip — and shouldn't.

Flagship tool: Residual Plot
Why we do this: Every prediction is a little bit wrong. Those little errors (residuals) should look like random noise, not a hidden pattern.
The logic: Plot every prediction error against its predicted value. If the model is doing its job, the errors should scatter randomly around zero with no shape.
What to look for: A random cloud of dots vs. a curve, a funnel, or a slope in the errors.
What it means: A shape in the residuals means the model is missing something real. A random cloud means it captured what it needed to.

What to check, and with what

AssumptionWhat it means in plain words
NormalityThe errors should roughly follow a bell curve
HomoscedasticityThe errors should stay about the same size across all predictions, not grow or shrink
LinearityThe true relationship should actually be a straight line, if that's the model you used
IndependenceOne person's error shouldn't be able to predict another's
MulticollinearityYour predictor variables shouldn't be secretly measuring the same thing
Influential observationsNo single extreme data point should be able to flip your whole conclusion

How good is the fit, overall?

NumberIn plain words
R² / Adjusted R²What share of the pattern your model actually explains, from 0 to 1
RMSE / MAEOn average, how far off the predictions are, in the original units
AIC / BICScorecards for comparing models — lower is better, and they penalize needless complexity
Hosmer-LemeshowChecks whether a yes/no model's predicted odds match what actually happened
ROC Curve / AUCHow well a yes/no model separates the two outcomes, from 0.5 (no better than guessing) to 1 (perfect)
Calibration plotsChecks whether "70% predicted" events really do happen about 70% of the time
