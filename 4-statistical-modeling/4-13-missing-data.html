Missing Data

The question: Some answers are missing — now what?

Almost every real dataset has gaps. What you do about them can quietly change your entire conclusion.

Flagship idea: Is the gap random?
Why we do this: Deleting incomplete rows is only safe if the missing answers are unrelated to anything you care about.
The logic: Check whether the people with missing answers look different from the people who answered — on age, group, outcome, anything.
What to look for: Is missingness scattered evenly, or does it cluster in one group (like sicker patients skipping a question)?
What it means: Random gaps are relatively safe to ignore. Gaps that cluster by group mean simply deleting those rows will bias your results.

Related tools

ToolIn plain words
Complete Case AnalysisSimply drop anyone with a missing answer — simple, but risky if gaps aren't random
Mean / Median ImputationFill every gap with the average — simple, but shrinks how much your data appears to vary
Hot DeckFill a gap by copying the answer from a similar respondent
Regression ImputationPredict the missing answer from the other things you know about that person
Multiple Imputation / MICEFill each gap several plausible ways, run the analysis on each, then combine the results honestly
FIML / EM AlgorithmAdvanced methods that use all available data directly, without ever filling in a single guessed value
