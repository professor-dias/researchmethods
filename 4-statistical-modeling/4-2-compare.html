The question: Are these groups actually different, or could that gap just be luck?

Any two groups will almost never have the exact same average. The real question is whether the gap you see is bigger than random luck alone would produce.

Flagship tool: The t-test
Why we do this: You have two groups (like Drug A vs. Placebo) and want to know if their averages are really different.
The logic: Look at the gap between the two group averages and compare it to how much each group naturally wobbles on its own. A gap that's big next to the natural wobble is convincing. A gap that's small next to the wobble could just be noise.
What to look for: A p-value, and how far apart the two curves below are.
What it means: A small p-value (below 0.05) means this gap would be surprising if there were truly no difference — so the groups are probably really different. Same coin-flip logic from Chapter 1, just with two groups instead of one.

SituationTest
Two independent groupsIndependent t-test
Same people, measured twicePaired t-test
Three or more groupsOne-way ANOVA
Groups split by two factors at once (e.g., Drug × Gender)Two-way ANOVA
Same people measured several timesRepeated Measures ANOVA
Comparing groups while controlling for another number (like age)ANCOVA
Comparing groups on several outcomes at onceMANOVA / MANCOVA
