Relate

The question: When one thing goes up, does the other go up too, go down, or do nothing?

This is about noticing a pattern between two things you measured on the same people — without claiming one causes the other.

Flagship tool: Correlation
Why we do this: You have two numbers per person (like height and weight) and want to know if they move together.
The logic: Plot every person as a dot, one variable on each side. Correlation is a single number from -1 to 1 that summarizes how tightly those dots line up.
What to look for: Do the dots form a clear line, or a shapeless cloud? Does the line tilt up or down?
What it means: Close to +1 means "both go up together." Close to -1 means "one goes up while the other goes down." Close to 0 means "no pattern at all." Correlation never tells you which one caused the other.

Choosing the right version

SituationTest

Ranked or not-quite-normal dataSpearman correlation
Small samples or lots of tied ranksKendall's Tau
Two categories vs. two categoriesChi-square test
Same, but very small samplesFisher's Exact Test
Same people, categories measured twiceMcNemar's Test
Same people, categories measured 3+ timesCochran's Q
