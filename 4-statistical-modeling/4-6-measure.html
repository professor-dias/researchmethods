Measure

The question: Am I actually measuring the thing I think I'm measuring?

Before you compare, relate, or predict anything, make sure your survey is measuring the real construct — not noise. You already built part of this in Chapter 2.

Flagship tool: Reliability (Cronbach's Alpha)
Why we do this: When several questions are supposed to measure the same thing (like anxiety), they should agree with each other.
The logic: Check how consistently people answer the related questions. If someone scores high on one anxiety question, do they tend to score high on the others too?
What to look for: A single number from 0 to 1 (alpha). Higher means the questions hang together well.
What it means: Above 0.70 is usually considered acceptable agreement. Below that, your "scale" might really be measuring several different things instead of one.
You already built a live example of this: revisit the composite anxiety score from Chapter 2 and notice how averaging four related questions into one score only makes sense if they're measuring the same underlying thing.
