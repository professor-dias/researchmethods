Longitudinal

The question: How does this change as time passes, and does it change differently for different groups?

Instead of one snapshot, you measure the same people again and again, and watch the story unfold.

Flagship tool: Growth Curve / Mixed Models
Why we do this: You measured the same people at several time points and want to know if they're changing, and whether two groups change at different speeds.
The logic: Draw each person's trend line over time, then average those trend lines within each group.
What to look for: Are the lines flat or sloped? Do the two group lines run parallel, or spread apart over time?
What it means: Parallel lines mean both groups change at the same rate. Lines that spread apart mean one group is changing faster — time itself is interacting with the group.

Group A (navy) and Group B (maroon), measured at four time points. Notice the lines pull apart — that spreading gap is the story.
