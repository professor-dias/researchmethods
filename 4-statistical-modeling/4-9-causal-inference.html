Causal Inference

The question: Did this actually cause that, not just happen alongside it?

Correlation and prediction can't tell you cause. These methods try to estimate what would have happened without the treatment, even though you can never actually observe that.

Flagship tool: Difference-in-Differences
Why we do this: One group gets a new policy or treatment, another doesn't, and you measure both before and after.
The logic: If the untreated group didn't change much, assume the treated group would have followed the same flat path too, if left alone. Compare what actually happened to that assumed path.
What to look for: The gap between where the treated group actually ended up, and where the untreated group's trend says they "should" have ended up.
What it means: That gap is your estimated effect of the treatment — as long as both groups really were on the same path before treatment started.

Control (navy) barely moves. Treated (maroon) jumps after treatment starts. The dashed line is the "what if nothing happened" guess — the gold gap is the estimated effect.

Related tools

ToolIn plain words
Propensity Score / Exact / Mahalanobis MatchingPairing up treated and untreated people who look nearly identical on everything else
IPTW / IPW, StandardizationRe-weighting your data so the treated and untreated groups look balanced overall
Interrupted Time SeriesWatching one long trend before and after a policy change, looking for a break
Regression DiscontinuityComparing people just above and just below a strict cutoff rule (like a test-score threshold)
Instrumental VariablesUsing a side factor that affects treatment but not the outcome directly, to untangle cause from coincidence
