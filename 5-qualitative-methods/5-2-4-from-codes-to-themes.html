Codes Are Bricks. Themes Are the Building.

By the end of 5.2.2 you might have 40, 60, or 100 individual codes scattered across your transcripts. None of those codes, on its own, is a finding. A theme is what emerges once you climb one level up: a pattern of shared meaning that runs across several codes, and often across several participants.

Notice the theme isn't just "Category 1 + Category 2" glued together — it's a higher-level idea that explains why those two categories keep showing up together. That explanatory step is the actual analytic work of this stage; it doesn't happen automatically just by grouping codes.

A Theme Is a Claim, Not a Label

The most common beginner mistake at this stage: a "theme" that's really just a topic name. A topic tells the reader what you talked about. A real theme tells them something you found.

Topic Label (not yet a theme)Real Theme
Example 1"Communication with providers""Patients disengage from care after their concerns go repeatedly unacknowledged"
Example 2"Medication side effects""Unmanaged side effects push patients toward silent self-medication rather than reporting them"
Why it mattersTells the reader the subject areaTells the reader an actual pattern — something they can agree, disagree with, or test further

A useful test: if you can't turn your theme name into a sentence with a subject and a claim, it's probably still a topic. Keep refining it until it says something.

Checking Themes Actually Fit the Data

Re-read every extract coded under a theme, together. Do they genuinely tell one coherent story, or did you force two different ideas into the same bucket?
Check the theme against the whole dataset, not just the extracts that inspired it. Does it hold up in transcripts you haven't looked at closely yet, or only in the two vivid quotes you remember?
Look for the exception, not just the pattern. A participant whose account doesn't fit the theme isn't a nuisance to drop — it's information about the theme's real boundaries, and often worth reporting explicitly.
Merge or split as needed. Two themes that keep overlapping in the same extracts are probably one theme; one theme that only holds together loosely may really be two.
