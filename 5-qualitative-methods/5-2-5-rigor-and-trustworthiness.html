Qualitative Work Has Its Own Rigor Vocabulary

2.3 gave you validity and threats to validity for quantitative work. Qualitative research answers the same underlying question — "can this be trusted?" — with a parallel vocabulary, laid out by Lincoln & Guba as four pillars of trustworthiness.
🎯
Credibility
Do the findings accurately represent participants' actual experience? The qualitative parallel to internal validity.
Strengthened by: triangulation, member checking
🌐
Transferability
Could a reader judge whether these findings apply to their own, different context? The qualitative parallel to external validity.
Strengthened by: thick, detailed description
📋
Dependability
Is the research process documented clearly enough that another researcher could follow it and understand every decision made?
Strengthened by: audit trails
🧠
Confirmability
Are the findings shaped by the data and participants, rather than by the researcher's own assumptions going unchecked?
Strengthened by: reflexivity, peer debriefing

Reflexivity: Turning the Lens on Yourself

Reflexivity is the ongoing practice of examining how your own identity, assumptions, and relationship to the topic shape what you notice, how participants respond to you, and how you interpret what they say. In qualitative work, you are part of the instrument — unlike a survey scale, you can't be "calibrated" away, so you have to be accounted for directly.

Positionality
Your specific standpoint relative to the topic and participants — your training, identity, prior experience, and any power difference between you and the people you're interviewing (e.g., clinician interviewing patients).
Usually stated directly, early, in the methods or a dedicated positionality statement — not hidden and not treated as irrelevant.
Reflexive Practice
A running reflexive journal kept throughout the project — not just at the end — noting reactions, assumptions being challenged, and moments where your own view might be steering the analysis.
Revisited explicitly during coding: "am I finding this because the data supports it, or because I expected to find it?"
Reflexivity isn't a confession you owe the reader once and move past — it's a discipline you practice throughout data collection and analysis, and a brief version of it belongs in your final write-up so a reader can weigh your findings with that context in mind.

Four Strategies, Each Doing Different Work

What It IsPillar It Strengthens
TriangulationChecking a finding against multiple data sources, methods, or investigators — e.g., interviews plus observation plus documents, or two researchers coding independently.Credibility
Member CheckingTaking your interpretation back to participants (or a subset of them) and asking: does this match your experience?Credibility
Peer DebriefingA colleague outside the project questions your process, interpretations, and blind spots — a structured devil's advocate.Confirmability
Audit TrailA documented record of every decision — sampling choices, codebook revisions, memos — detailed enough that someone else could retrace your reasoning.Dependability
