A Paradigm, Not a Method

Community-based participatory research (CBPR) doesn't name a design or an analysis technique — a CBPR study can use any design from 2.2 and any analysis from Topic 3 or 5. What makes it CBPR is who holds the power at every stage: the community affected by the issue is a genuine partner in defining the question, running the study, and using the findings — not a data source the researcher passes through on the way to a publication.

How the Pieces Fit Together

A widely used conceptual model (Wallerstein & Duran) frames CBPR as a logic chain: the starting conditions shape the partnership, the partnership shapes the research itself, and good research done this way produces outcomes that go beyond a publication.

Contexts: The starting conditions — community history, culture, prior experience with researchers, existing trust or mistrust, local capacity.
Group dynamics & equitable partnership: Structural (formal agreements, shared resources), individual (trust, motivation, cultural humility), and relational (safety, listening, shared decision-making) factors that determine whether the partnership is genuinely equitable.
Intervention & research: A culturally grounded intervention and an appropriate research design (2.2), shaped jointly rather than imposed.
Health & system outcomes: Not just findings — policy change, community empowerment, new skills on both sides, and (the ultimate goal) reduced health disparities.

How Much Power Does "Participatory" Actually Mean?

Not every project calling itself community-based gives the community real influence. A useful gradient (Hatch et al.) ranks four levels from least to most community control.

1. Periphery consultation
Least
2. Passive endorsement
 
3. Community-based, not community-involved
 
4. True CBPR
Most
At level 3, community residents might be hired as interviewers or outreach workers — real jobs, but no say in the research question or interpretation. Only at level 4 are community representatives "first among equals" in defining the agenda and negotiating how findings get used. Conflict at that level is expected, not a sign something's gone wrong — it's negotiated, not avoided by finding a more agreeable site.

What CBPR Actually Buys You

Development
Research questions the community actually finds meaningful.
Academic theory merged with lay knowledge — often a richer understanding than either alone.
Implementation
More culturally appropriate methods and measures — often better reliability and validity as a result.
Higher recruitment and retention, driven by real trust rather than incentive payments alone.
Dissemination
Richer interpretation of findings from people who live the context.
Faster translation into actual practice and policy change.

Where CBPR Gets Hard

Eight real challenges, and what experienced teams do about them 

Time: Real partnerships take longer to build than a funding deadline allows. Start relationships before a grant opportunity exists, not after.

Sharing power over the budget: Not just token consultation — co-develop the budget, and help community partners learn to administer grant funds directly.

Shared authorship and data ownership: Decide this up front, and train community partners in the publication process so it's a real choice, not a formality.

IRB and human-subjects training: Every community member doing human-subjects work needs to be listed on the protocol and certified — the same standard as any researcher.

Disagreement on which problem to target: Bring in more resources or expertise rather than one side simply overriding the other.

Community priorities vs. funder requirements: Review the funding announcement together with the community before committing to what gets proposed.

Resistance to a specific design (e.g., withholding treatment from a control group): Explain the trade-offs honestly, and offer real alternatives — a wait-list control preserves scientific rigor while addressing the concern.

Unfamiliar community context or internal conflict: Engage long before any grant exists — stakeholder meetings, forums, and simply showing up matter more than any single formal step.
